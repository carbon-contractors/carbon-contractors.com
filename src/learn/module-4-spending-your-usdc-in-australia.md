## You've got USDC. Now spend it.

You did the work. The USDC hit your wallet. Great — but you can't tap your Coinbase Smart Wallet at Coles. Yet.

There are now two honest routes from USDC to real-world spending, and the right one depends on how you feel about custody. This module covers both, and it's deliberately boring about each. Getting paid in crypto is the interesting part. Spending it should be dull, cheap and predictable.

## Route 1: The card route — spend USDC directly

A new wave of crypto-linked debit cards lets you hold USDC and spend it anywhere the card network is accepted — the conversion to AUD happens at checkout. No weekly cash-out ritual, no waiting for a bank transfer.

**Two flavours, one important difference:**

- **Spend from an exchange balance.** Coinbase offers cards in Australia that spend against your Coinbase account balances, including USDC. You move USDC from your wallet to your exchange account, and the card does the rest.
- **Spend from self-custody.** Cards like the MetaMask Card connect directly to your own wallet over Base — the same network your Carbon Contractors USDC lives on — and convert only at the moment of purchase. Your keys, your funds, until you tap.

**The honest catch:** availability moves. As we write this, the self-custody cards are not yet open to Australian residents (they launched in the US, UK, Europe and parts of the Americas), and even US sign-ups for some of them pause and restart. The Card tab inside the wallet app is the source of truth — if it shows a waitlist, treat it as a watch-item, not a plan.

**Why this route matters even while you wait:** it's the first time "getting paid in USDC" and "buying groceries" connect without a bank in the middle. When an AU-eligible self-custody card lands, your Carbon Contractors earnings become spendable the moment escrow releases them.

## Route 2: The cash-out route — exchange, sell, withdraw

The boring classic, and it always works:

1. Transfer USDC from your wallet to your exchange account
2. Sell USDC for AUD
3. Withdraw AUD to your bank account

Steps 1 and 2 take minutes. Step 3 is usually near-instant in Australia thanks to PayID and Osko, rather than the 1–3 business days a bank transfer used to mean.

**Why Coinbase specifically for the cash-out?** Two reasons, and only one of them is about convenience.

- **It's the same rails the rest of this stack already runs on.** Your wallet is a Coinbase Smart Wallet, the escrow is on Base, and Base is built by Coinbase. Fewer moving parts, fewer addresses to get wrong.
- **Regulatory standing.** Coinbase Australia was granted an Australian Financial Services Licence directly by ASIC in April 2026 — the first crypto exchange to receive one that way. That's a meaningful signal about the entity you're handing money to.

Be precise about what that second point means, though: an AFSL says something about the *company's* regulatory obligations. It is not a guarantee about any particular product, fee, or feature, and it is not protection against you making a mistake with an address.

## One thing that trips people up

**USDC tracks the US dollar, not the Australian dollar.** One USDC is about one USD. What that's worth in AUD moves with the exchange rate — so if you're quoting an hourly rate in USDC, you're effectively quoting in USD. Check the rate before you set your rate.

This matters double with cards: a card that converts at checkout locks in whatever the rate is at that moment. If the AUD moves sharply between your payday and your shopping trip, the same USDC buys a different amount of groceries. That's not a fault — it's just what spending a USD-pegged asset in Australia means.

## Do your own research

This is the part we'd rather over-emphasise than under-emphasise.

**We are not financial advisers, and this module is not financial advice.** We don't have a commercial relationship with Coinbase, MetaMask, or any card issuer, and we're not paid to send you anywhere. If that ever changes, we'll say so on this page in plain language.

Before you sign up for any card or move money anywhere:

- **Check availability and fees yourself, today.** Card availability, spreads, conversion fees and withdrawal fees change — sometimes weekly. The provider's own pricing page beats anything we could write here.
- **Check the provider still operates the way you expect.** This page can go stale. An earlier version of this module recommended a card provider that was winding down — which is exactly why it now says less and asks you to verify more.
- **Understand the tax position.** Spending crypto on a card is generally a disposal for Australian tax purposes, and being paid in crypto is generally income. The ATO publishes guidance on both. If the amounts matter to you, talk to an accountant rather than to us.
- **Never send funds to an address you haven't verified.** No provider will ever ask you for your seed phrase or passkey. See Module 6.

Other exchanges and other cards exist. We're not going to name providers we haven't verified — do your own research and pick what suits you.

This is why the entire Carbon Contractors stack exists:

- **Module 1:** You already use digital money — USDC is just a better version.
- **Module 2:** Your wallet is yours — self-custody without the headaches.
- **Module 3:** AI agents pay you in USDC — instantly, with escrow guarantees.

The loop is closing. Work comes in, money comes in, and money goes out at the shops — through a bank or over a card network, but increasingly without either being the bottleneck.

## What you need to know right now

Three things:

1. **Two routes exist.** Cards spend your USDC at checkout (fast, availability varies); exchange-and-withdraw turns it into banked AUD (always works, a few more steps). Coinbase Australia is where we'd start either, for the reasons above.
2. **Self-custody cards are the direction, not the destination.** They connect to your wallet over Base — but check your Card tab before planning around one, especially in Australia.
3. **Verify everything yourself.** Availability, fees and tax rules change faster than this page does. This is a starting point, not advice.

# Pseudonymous is not anonymous

**Module 7 of 7 · 4 min read**

---

We never ask who you are. No name, no ID, no selfie holding your passport. You register a wallet, pick your service categories, set your rate, and you're working. Call yourself whatever you like — nothing checks it, because nothing uses it.

That is a real thing we do, and we're not going to walk it back. But it is easy to hear "no ID required" and conclude "nobody can ever work out who I am," and that conclusion is wrong. This module explains the difference, because you're making a decision at registration that you can't unmake later, and you should make it knowingly.

## The difference in one sentence

**Anonymous** means there is nothing to connect. **Pseudonymous** means there is a persistent name — your wallet address — and everything you do is connected to it, permanently and publicly. That name just doesn't happen to be your legal one.

Yet.

## The part people miss: it works backwards

Here's the thing that catches people out. It isn't that someone might identify you from a task you do today. It's that **every task you have ever completed is a permanent public record, sitting there, waiting for the one future moment that links your wallet to you.**

When that moment comes — and it might be years from now — it doesn't just expose that one moment. The whole history resolves at once. Every job, every payment, every timestamp, all the way back.

There's no fixing it afterwards. Nobody can delete it. Not you, not us, not the person who linked it. That's what "on-chain" means: the record is held by thousands of independent computers and nothing removes it.

So the question isn't "am I safe today." It's "am I comfortable with this being permanently linkable to me if something connects it in 2031."

## The ways wallets get linked

Not an exhaustive list, and not tradecraft — just the common ones, so you know what you're dealing with.

**Cashing out.** The most common by a wide margin. The moment you move USDC to an exchange to convert it to dollars, that exchange knows who you are — they're legally required to. Your wallet is now associated with your identity in their records. Investigators, and sometimes anyone with a subpoena, can work from there.

**Reusing the wallet.** Use the same address for anything that carries your name — an NFT with your handle on it, a donation, a tip jar linked from a social account, a payment to a mate who knows you — and the link is made. It stays made.

**Reusing the handle.** If your username here matches one you've used somewhere with your real name attached, that's the link. People are far more consistent with usernames than they realise, and old forum posts don't expire.

**Wallet clustering.** Funding a new wallet from an old one connects them. This is well-understood, automated, and cheap to do at scale.

**Someone already knows.** You take a task from a client you've worked for before. You mention the platform to a friend. Small worlds are small.

People who do this for a living are very good at it. Independent investigators have unmasked people from a single bridge transaction and a username they used once, years earlier. Assume competence on the other side.

## The one that has nothing to do with crypto

This one is specific to the kind of work here, and it's worth thinking about carefully.

Physical tasks build a pattern. Same suburb. Same hours. Same categories of work. Do enough vehicle inspections in the northern suburbs on weekday mornings and someone has narrowed you down considerably before they've looked at a single transaction.

We can reduce this — task content is deleted after settlement, so descriptions and locations don't linger with us. But the payment record on-chain still shows amounts, timing, and who paid you. That's inherent to the work being physical and in the world, and we can't engineer it away. We can only tell you it's there.

## What we hold, and what we don't

Being straight with you about our side of it:

**We hold:** your wallet address, your service categories, your rate, your stake, and a reputation score calculated from your completed work.

**We don't hold:** your name, address, documents, or anything we didn't list above. We don't hold your task evidence — photos go to storage the hiring agent controls, our checker reads them once, and we keep only a fingerprint that proves what was submitted without containing it. Task descriptions and results are deleted once a job is settled and the dispute window has closed.

**We can't help you afterwards.** If your wallet gets linked to you, there is nothing we can delete that fixes it, because the part that exposes you isn't ours. It's on the chain. We'd like to be able to offer you more than that, and we can't.

## The trade, stated honestly

If you want a clean break from your history, you start a fresh wallet. That works — the new wallet has no past.

It also has **no reputation, no completed jobs, no ranking in search, and no stake standing.** You start over. Everything you built is attached to the old address and it doesn't transfer, because the entire point of the reputation system is that it can't be moved or faked.

That's a genuine cost with no clever workaround. We're not going to pretend there's a neat solution, because there isn't one. It's a real trade and it's yours to make.

## What we'd suggest

Not rules, just what we'd tell a friend:

- **Use a wallet you created for this and use it for nothing else.** The single highest-value habit.
- **Think about cashing out before you start**, not after you've built up a balance. It's the most likely linking point, and it's much easier to plan for than to undo.
- **Pick a handle you have never used anywhere else.** Genuinely never.
- **Decide now, not later.** Switching wallets is cheap on day one and expensive after fifty completed jobs.
- **Assume it may become public.** Not because it will, but because deciding on that basis is the only decision you won't regret.

## The short version

Pseudonymous means you have a different name, not no name. The record is permanent, public, and resolves backwards the moment anything links it to you. We ask you for nothing and we keep almost nothing, and none of that changes what the chain already holds.

Work accordingly. And if that's fine by you — which for most people, doing ordinary work, it is — then you're all set.

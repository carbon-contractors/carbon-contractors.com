# CC-091 — Succession and disaster recovery

`docs/Key-Compromise-Recovery.md` covers the key being in the **wrong** hands. This ticket covers the key
being in **nobody's** hands — the founder being deceased, incapacitated, or permanently unreachable
(the "QuadrigaCX archetype").

## Architectural Resolution (`ADR-0006`)

This design investigation graduated to **`ADR-0006` (Continuity, succession, and the right to fork)** on
2026-08-19. The full operational procedures are consolidated in **`docs/runbooks/DISASTER-RECOVERY-AND-SUCCESSION.md`**.

## Update 2026-08-26 — `ADR-0006` accepted; this stays open on custody, not on design

`ADR-0006` is **accepted** with execution parameters, which closes the design question this ticket
raised ("this probably graduates to an ADR" — it did). What shipped in the same change:

- **D1** — copyright retained by **Aaron James Clifft personally**. `LICENSE` (AGPL-3.0-or-later),
  `contracts/LICENSE` (MIT, so the carve-out is a file rather than only an SPDX header), and
  `COMMERCIAL.md` moved from draft to in-force for the AGPL grant.
- **D5 / D7** — `docs/BCP-DR.md` expanded and **`chain-constants.json` created in-repo**. No
  dependency on a private workspace, which was the stated flaw in the previous DR plan.
- **D8** — backup posture decided: registration data is backed up, **task content and evidence are
  not**, so `privacy.md`'s deletion guarantee survives a restore. Requirement accepted; the Supabase
  configuration that satisfies it is unbuilt.
- **D2 / D3** — scoped into `CC-034` and `CC-090`.

### Why this issue does not close

The thing it was actually about is still open:

- **Custody of the three multisig keys.** The architecture is settled; who holds the cards is not.
  Three cards in one person's custody is *loss resistance*, not *succession* — an estate cannot reach
  a key it cannot find, and it certainly cannot reach two of three. This is the entire residual.
- **The re-host runbook** (D7) — `chain-constants.json` tells a stranger *what* the values are;
  nothing yet tells them *what to do with them*.
- **The D8 restore test** — a backup configured wrong is indistinguishable from one configured right
  until somebody restores.
- **Whether any of this needs to exist in law rather than in code.** Out of engineering scope and
  unanswered.

Stays `in-progress`. Closing it now would record succession as handled when what has actually been
handled is the design and the paperwork.

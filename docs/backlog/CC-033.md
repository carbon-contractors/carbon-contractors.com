# CC-033 — Requester gas stake

Carried over from Linear NOR-183, the last remaining item on the pre-mainnet list that was
still open. Classified in the archived architecture doc as an architectural improvement
rather than a hard blocker.

Requesters should post a gas stake so that abandoned or spammed tasks are not subsidised by
the platform signer.

Needs a design pass before implementation: how much, refunded when, and how it interacts
with the x402 funding path.

---

## Update 2026-07-25 — full detail from the Linear export

Promoted to P1. The export reframes this: it is not a nice-to-have, it converts an operational
liability into a product feature and it **supersedes** NOR-181 (gas balance monitoring), which
was cancelled on that basis.

### Design
The requester stakes the task value plus a small gas buffer (~$0.50-1 USDC) at task creation.
The buffer swaps to ETH and tops up the platform gas pool.

**Forfeit failsafe:** if the pool is empty, the task value releases directly from escrow with no
platform wallet involvement. The system degrades to still-paying-the-worker, which is the right
failure mode.

### Why it is worth more than it looks
- Requester has skin in the game, which deters spam and abandonment
- Self-healing operations — the gas pool refills itself, so CC-040 no longer needs a deployer
  ETH balance alert
- Trust signal to workers
- A micro-revenue model, if the buffer is set slightly above actual gas cost

### MVP simplification
Accept the ETH buffer separately from the USDC and skip the on-chain swap. Shared pool rather
than task-scoped.

Related: CC-040 (the monitoring this removes the need for), CC-039.

## Update 2026-08-13 — partly overtaken by `ADR-0001` Amendment 1

The gas economics this issue addresses have changed shape. Post-Amendment 1 **the platform makes no
transaction in the settlement path at all** — verdicts are EIP-712 signatures handed to the parties,
and the worker claims their own payment paying their own gas.

So the "abandoned tasks drain the platform signer's gas" premise largely dissolves for settlement.
What survives:

- Gas is still spent on **deployment and ownership operations**.
- The **spam-deterrent** argument for a requester stake is untouched, and is arguably strengthened
  now that `CC-081` Defect 4 shows `request_human_work` is unauthenticated and can create unlimited
  task rows.
- The **micro-revenue** angle is unchanged, and interacts with `ADR-0002` D8: introducing a platform
  fee strengthens the "trades in personal information for a benefit" limb, so it is a decision with
  a regulatory dimension, not only a pricing one.

Worth re-reading this ticket's design section against Amendment 1 before implementing — the
"buffer swaps to ETH and tops up the platform gas pool" mechanism is solving a problem that is now
much smaller.

Priority stays **P1**, but the justification has narrowed.

Related: `ADR-0001` Amendment 1, `ADR-0002` D8, `CC-040`, `CC-081` Defect 4.

## Closed `wontfix` — 2026-09-16 — design pass complete; the stake solves a problem the architecture no longer has

The 2026-08-11 triage pass left one gate on this ticket: a design pass re-reading the original
design against Amendment 1 before implementing. That pass ran today against the current tree
(`60b7407`, post-`CC-082` contract, post-Defect-1-fix funding path). It closes the ticket rather
than scopes an implementation, on five findings.

### 1 — The gas-subsidy premise has dissolved

The ticket's founding premise — *abandoned or spammed tasks are subsidised by the platform
signer* — no longer describes the system. `ADR-0001` Amendment 1 (2026-08-13) removed the
platform from the settlement path entirely:

- Verdicts are EIP-712 signatures handed to the parties — **no gas**.
- `releaseAfterReview` and `expireTask` are party-claimed pull-payments (`A1.2`) — **the
  platform pays no claim gas**.
- `CC-081` Defect 1's fix (2026-08-21) made the agent fund from its own wallet via
  `USDC.approve` + `escrow.createTask` — **the platform fronts no funding gas**. `createTask` is
  a `safeTransferFrom(msg.sender, …)` (`CarbonEscrow.sol:316`): a spammer self-pays their own
  `approve` + `createTask` gas, per task, forever.

An abandoned task now costs the requester their own gas and their principal locked to the
deadline — the requester **is** staked, in the strongest form available to the architecture.
`expireTask` refunds the principal, but the gas and the time-value lock are gone, and a spam
campaign of scale needs its own wallet-farming operation before rate limits bind.

### 2 — The original mechanism would be a step backwards

The 2026-07-25 design — a $0.50–1 USDC buffer swapped to ETH into a platform gas pool —
would reintroduce exactly what Amendment 1 spent its authority removing:

- **A platform-held ETH pool is custody.** The pool needs an owner-withdraw or sweep path,
  and every such path is a `CC-051` surface. The platform holds no funds, manages no funds —
  that constraint predates this design and outlives it.
- **A swap component is a new trust+supply-chain surface** (oracle or pool dependency, price
  manipulation, an extra contract to audit) — all to solve the settlement-gas problem, and
  there is no settlement gas.
- **Platform gas pool → platform liveness in every task's lifecycle.** Gas pool empties →
  tasks can't settle → monitoring obligation → a `CC-040`/`ADR-0003` watch item that
  Amendment 1 deleted by design. Reinstating it is the reverse of the direction of travel.

### 3 — The "forfeit failsafe" is the shipped design, not a failsafe

The design's own fallback — *if the pool is empty, release from escrow with no platform wallet
involvement, degrading to still-paying-the-worker* — is not a degraded mode in the current
architecture. **It is the architecture.** Settlement is party-paid pull-payment with a
worker-default liveness guarantee (D6 + A1.2); the platform has no settlement transaction to
subsidise or withhold. The failsafe was a hedge against the gas-pool mechanism's failure mode;
removing the mechanism removes the need for the hedge.

### 4 — Spam deterrence is already structurally covered, by cheaper means

Post-Defect-4 (2026-08-16) the cost profile of task creation is already the skin-in-the-game
this ticket wanted:

- **Authentication**: `request_human_work` requires `context.callerWallet`; provenance can't be
  forged (it is the field every downstream authorisation check trusts).
- **Paid inputs**: `acceptance_spec` is required (a spec costs an LLM call to author), and
  `to_human_wallet` is validated against the `humans` table.
- **Rate limit**: 30/hour per authenticated wallet (`taskCreationRateLimiter`), Upstash-backed
  since `CC-020` PR #210 (2026-09-16, hub state).
- **On-chain cost floor**: `approve` + `createTask` on Base — the spammer's own gas.
  Note: the app sets **no minimum task value** (`amount_usdc` is `positive()` with no floor),
  so cost-per-row is gas + principal lock + LLM-authored spec, not a burned deposit. Honest
  statement of the residual: a determined spammer still pays only gas + time-value lock to
  create a `pending` row. With `acceptance_spec` required, `specHash == 0` tasks can't be
  created through the product at all, so "abandoned task → worker wins by default" via empty
  spec is closed at intake, not at settlement.

A $0.50–1 buffer on top of that stack is a strictly weaker gate than the stack already applies,
and it burdens every legitimate requester to defend against a residual the stack already prices.

### 5 — Micro-revenue is a fee decision, and it is deliberately not made

The 2026-07-25 export framed the buffer as a possible micro-revenue stream. In the current
architecture any platform take is a **platform fee**, which is a regulatory trigger, not a
pricing knob: `ADR-0002` D8 records that monetisation is *the* trigger to watch (it materially
strengthens the "trades in personal information for a benefit" limb of the Privacy Act
exception), and A1.2 records that the fee model deliberately does not exist and is a
decision reserved for counsel ("raise before monetising, not after"). An engineering ticket
cannot make that decision; it is not this ticket's to make. If a fee is ever introduced it gets
its own ticket and its own ADR, with counsel input — it does not arrive by letting a gas buffer
drift above cost.

### Disposition

- The gas-stake mechanism: **not built** — the problem (platform-subsidised gas) was removed by
  `ADR-0001` Amendment 1 and the `CC-081` fixes, not by a stake.
- The spam-deterrent goal: **achieved by other means** (authentication + required spec +
  rate limit + self-paid gas + principal lock), and priced honestly above.
- The micro-revenue goal: **out of scope for engineering** — it is the `ADR-0002` D8 fee
  decision, deliberately deferred and requiring counsel.
- The operational liability this ticket superseded `CC-040`'s deployer ETH balance alert over:
  **moot for settlement** (recorded in `CC-040` 2026-08-13 and `ADR-0003`), and remains
  relevant only for deployment/ownership operations — a monitoring concern, not a stake design.

No pull-request can make this ticket more done than the architecture already has. Closing as
`wontfix`: **superseded by design**, with the design pass recorded here so it is never
re-derived from the Linear export's framing again.

# CC-098 — AUSTRAC AML/CTF Tranche 2 (VASP reform) — a second, faster-moving regime `CC-051` does not cover

## Why this is a separate ticket, not an update to `CC-051`

`CC-051` tracks one regime: the **Corporations Amendment (Digital Assets Framework) Bill 2025** —
an ASIC/AFSL licensing question, "Digital Asset Platform" (DAP) category, commences **9 April
2027**, with a small-scale exemption (annual volume < $10m **and** < $5,000 held per client) that
puts Carbon Contractors comfortably outside it today per that ticket's own reasoning.

That is a different law from the one below, administered by a different regulator, on a
different clock. Folding this into `CC-051` would bury a nearer deadline inside a ticket whose
entire conclusion is "21 months of runway" — which is only true for the regime `CC-051` actually
analysed.

**Not legal advice.** Same posture as `CC-051` and `ADR-0002` D8: this is a flag from a code/doc
review, scoped so a lawyer can be asked the sharp question instead of a vague one.

## The regime

The **AML/CTF Tranche 2 reform** amends the *Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorism
Financing Act 2006* to bring "virtual asset service providers" (VASPs) into full scope, alongside
tranche 2 sectors more broadly (lawyers, accountants, real estate, etc.). Administered by
AUSTRAC, not ASIC.

Key dates, from AUSTRAC's own reform guidance (`austrac.gov.au/amlctf-reform`):

- **31 March 2026** — reform commences. Existing DCE-registered entities are auto-converted to
  VASP registration; new entrants providing a registrable virtual asset service from this date
  have **28 days** to enrol.
- **1 July 2026** — full compliance obligations commence for VASPs: AML/CTF program, customer
  due diligence, sanctions/PEP screening, suspicious matter reporting, threshold transaction
  reporting, 7-year record-keeping, a nominated AML/CTF Compliance Officer.

**Both dates have already passed as of this writing (2026-08-18).** There is no multi-year
runway here the way there is in `CC-051` — if Carbon Contractors' escrow arrangement is a
registrable virtual asset service, the obligation window is open now, not in 2027.

**The saving fact, same shape as `CC-081`/`CC-037`'s "nothing lost because nothing has run"
reasoning:** the site is behind the coming-soon gate, `/api/fund-task` has never successfully
funded a task (`CC-081` Defect 1), and no designated service has actually been provided to a
member of the public. A registrable service has to be *provided* before the obligation to be
registered bites in practice — so today's exposure is "not yet triggered", not "already
breached". That window closes the moment `CC-039`'s mainnet smoke test runs, even self-funded,
if the activity performed meets the definition below. This needs resolving **before** `CC-039`,
not during it.

## Why Carbon Contractors is arguably in scope

The Act's Table 1 virtual-asset designated services include (item numbers per AUSTRAC's own
published list — worth confirming wording against the current Rules before relying on it):

- exchanging virtual assets for money, or arranging such exchange
- exchanging virtual assets for other virtual assets
- Tranche 2's expansion additionally reaches **custodial wallet / safekeeping services** and
  **arranging or facilitating a transfer of virtual assets on behalf of another person**

`CarbonEscrow` holds USDC (a virtual asset under this framework) locked under contract logic
until a release condition is met, and directs its destination between two parties fixed at
funding. Two readings compete, and both need a lawyer, not a guess:

- **Against:** the platform never has a private key that can move the funds anywhere but the two
  fixed addresses (`ADR-0001` D9) — the same non-custodial argument `CC-051` already makes for the
  ASIC question. Deterministic-contract release is arguably not "safekeeping" in the sense the Act
  means, since no human discretion decides which wallet holds the token day to day.
- **For:** the platform deploys and owns the contract, and `resolveDispute` gives the owner
  discretion over which of the two fixed destinations receives funds in a dispute — which looks
  like "arranging a transfer of virtual assets on behalf of another person" for at least the
  disputed subset of transactions, and the verdict-signing role (`ADR-0001` D9) is the platform
  exercising authority over settlement even in the non-disputed path.

The DAP small-scale exemption from `CC-051` **does not transfer to this Act** — the AML/CTF Act's
designated-service test is about *what service is provided*, not transaction volume. There is no
equivalent "you're too small to count" carve-out found in the research so far; confirm this
directly with counsel rather than assuming it.

## The `ADR-0002` D1 tension, named so it isn't missed

`ADR-0002` D1 commits to "pseudonymous by design; no identity verification, ever" and
`README.md:11` advertises "No KYC, no resumes, no interviews." If this ticket resolves to "in
scope", full customer due diligence (name, DOB, address verification for individuals) is a
standard AML/CTF program element and is very hard to reconcile with D1 as written.

Two things soften this, worth raising with the lawyer rather than assuming either without
confirmation:

- AUSTRAC's regime generally permits **simplified due diligence for low ML/TF-risk customers**,
  which a microtask marketplace with capped per-task value may qualify for — this would not
  require full identity verification, only proportionate checks.
- **Sanctions/PEP screening is address-based, not identity-based** (`CC-099`). It does not require
  knowing who a wallet belongs to, only whether that address appears on a published list. This is
  compatible with `ADR-0002` D1 as written and should proceed regardless of how the CDD question
  resolves.

If full CDD does turn out to be required, `README.md`'s "No KYC" claim becomes a claim that
cannot be true under `ADR-0004`'s own test ("a claim that will never be true... is still a real
defect") and needs correcting alongside the architecture change, not after it ships.

## Update 2026-08-18 — who is "the customer" is itself ambiguous, and it sharpens the D1 tension

There is no single AML/CTF Act definition of "customer" — it is defined per designated service in
section 6's tables, and AUSTRAC's own published guidance points two different ways on two-party
transactions, neither of which maps cleanly onto this platform:

- **Real estate brokering** (the closest analogy to a two-sided marketplace): AUSTRAC states the
  customer is **both** the seller/transferor and the buyer/transferee.
- **A solicitor holding a deposit on trust**: the customer is **only** the solicitor's own
  instructing client, not the counterparty who paid the deposit into trust.

If the first analogy applies, **both the hiring agent and the human worker** are customers, which
means CDD would reach worker registration directly — a much sharper collision with `ADR-0002` D1
than if only agents needed it (D1 is specifically the worker-facing "no identity verification,
ever" commitment). If the second applies, only the agent is a customer and D1 survives largely
intact. This is squarely a question for the lawyer already scoped above — hand over both AUSTRAC
precedents rather than assuming either.

**Aaron's counter-argument, added 2026-08-18:** the real-estate-brokering precedent describes a
broker paid a fee for arranging the two-sided transaction. Carbon Contractors currently charges
**no platform fee** — no personal revenue accrual is being taken until the project proves viable.
The argument: a fee-free platform may not sit on the same footing as a paid broker for deciding
which side of the transaction is "the customer," which would favour the narrower,
agent-only reading.

**The half that will need testing with counsel:** AUSTRAC's guidance elsewhere is that even
occasional, non-commercial-feeling provision of a designated service can trigger obligations once
an activity is "carrying on a business" — so the fee point likely narrows *who counts as a
customer* rather than removing the designated-service question outright. Brief the lawyer on both
the argument and this limit. Full treatment recorded in `ADR-0002` Amendment 1, alongside the
decision that D1 (no identity verification) stands for now regardless of how this classification
lands — that decision does not wait on this ticket.
